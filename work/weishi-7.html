Wesee 7.0: a video editor that opens up in stages.

Keep everyday editing approachable, while giving creators a clear path to more precise control.

01 / Different levels of editing

One publishing flow had to serve very different habits.

Some people wanted to make a quick everyday video; others wanted precise control over text, effects, and timing. Putting every control on the same level could overwhelm the first group or constrain the second.

I led the design team in finding a structure for both: a clear basic publishing path, with more advanced tools available when the task required them.

02 / Open the editor in stages

Keep the first steps focused, then reveal more craft.

We organized editing into three levels. The first two covered the core tasks for simpler videos; the third offered advanced controls. Across these levels, we reused three panel patterns: editor home, selection, and track or timeline.

03 / Make timing the next step

Adding text was not the end of the task.

In the earlier flow, users returned to the editor home after adding text, where timing controls were harder to find. Task validation showed that they expected to adjust duration immediately after entering text.

We made text entry lead directly to duration adjustment, then applied the same interaction to stickers and effects, where creators might also need to set when an element appears.

04 / Keep music control flexible

Make the simple choice familiar; leave room for precision.

For basic music selection, we replaced a hard-to-discover waveform gesture with a familiar progress bar. Timeline editing and fades remained available for more precise control, while lyric feedback helped users locate a passage as they adjusted a music segment.

An approachable first action let people finish a simple edit, while a more precise set of tools remained available when they needed it.
