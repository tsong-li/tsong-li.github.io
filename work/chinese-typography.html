Localizing Microsoft’s typography for Chinese.

I developed multilingual typography proposals, coordinated Chinese font-switching plans across product teams, and helped designers work with language-specific font behavior.

01 / Understand the mismatch

Matching numbers can produce very different type.

Chinese text in Discover Feeds needed to work alongside Ginto. Using the same size, weight, and line height across scripts could leave Chinese headings looking heavy, paragraphs crowded, and mixed text unevenly aligned.

I examined weight, apparent character size, line height, letter spacing, and baseline alignment. Comparisons of characters, phrases, and paragraphs helped turn those visual differences into specific design decisions.

02 / Choose the pairing

Choose the font and its settings together.

I explored adjustments to Microsoft YaHei, then compared it with Noto Sans SC alongside Ginto. The study considered whether a broader choice of weights and different glyph proportions could produce a more balanced pairing.

The design proposal used Noto Sans SC and considered different spacing needs for interface labels, summaries, and body text. The aim was to support both quick scanning and longer reading.

03 / Compare in context

See how the type works across a whole page.

I brought the typography choices back into Discover Feeds and article layouts. The before-and-after designs show how the changes affect card headings, source labels, article titles, body text, and Chinese–Latin text within the same line.

04 / Coordinate the switch

Connect the design proposal to the product teams.

I developed the multilingual typography proposals and worked with the Windows, Office, and Edge teams on plans to switch Chinese-language experiences to Noto Sans CN, supplied with Windows 11 and Edge.

I also joined technical discussions with FounderType about updates to Microsoft YaHei’s font weights.

05 / Share the principles

Give designers a way to reason about multilingual type.

I shared multilingual typography principles with internal design teams and developed font fallback guidance. The sessions explained how writing systems differ, how browsers choose fonts for individual characters, and why the same weight value can look different across fonts.

The guidance also addressed regional CJK glyph forms and language-specific font choices. It gave teams concrete comparisons to use when evaluating typography in their own interfaces.
