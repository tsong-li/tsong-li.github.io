Light Studio / Tencent Effect: make technical setup feel like creative work.

Organize a complex effect-production workflow around the way creative teams build, adjust, and preview their work.

01 / Start with the working reality

Creative production crossed too many handoffs.

Effect production involved creative work, technical configuration, and operations. Designers exchanged files, edited JSON to tune effects, and used a complex content-management system. These steps could distract from the creative work.

I led the design team on Light Studio / Tencent Effect, a production tool for the Wesee ecosystem. We needed to make the workflow easier to understand while keeping the technical relationships behind each effect visible.

02 / Build a familiar workspace

Give each kind of work a place people can recognize.

We drew on established creative tools to give tools and layers, the scene, parameters, timeline, resources, and preview stable places in the workspace. Different creative disciplines could recognize these regions, which left room for the range of effect work the tool needed to support.

03 / Put technical relationships in human terms

Let creators work with effects, not renderer vocabulary.

Effects could contain nested, rendered assets, whose relationships were hard to understand in generic folders. We explored connected nodes and hierarchical groups, using names such as effects, probability, transitions, and stickers. The aim was to help creators organize their work without requiring renderer terminology.

04 / Recover context and preview

Keep people oriented as they move between tools.

During long editing sessions, creators could lose track of the selected element. We worked on showing the corresponding selection across panels so they could return to it without searching again.

LightFlow, the companion to Light Studio, also provided phone previews. Creators could check an effect in a more relevant setting while continuing to work on it.

05 / What the work made possible

Reduce the translation between creative intent and configuration.

A video designer reported being able to adjust effects without editing JSON directly. This was a concrete example of the intended change: less time spent manually translating a creative idea into technical settings.

The case combined workspace structure, clear effect relationships, selection recovery, and previews in context, giving creators a more familiar way to work with the technical complexity that remained.
